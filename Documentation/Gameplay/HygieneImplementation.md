# Restaurant hygiene implementation and external acceptance

Implemented service-system defaults:

- Equipment and dining surfaces have independent, stable dirt identities. Successful kitchen station work adds 0.05 by default; counters receive 20% of that rate and a lighter overlay. Unused surfaces remain clean. Room warnings use the highest local dirt value: warning at 0.6, unsanitary at 1.0.
- Seating/service phases, eating, bill delivery, payment and dirty trays add wear to their own booth or table. Table cleanup clears that surface; washing its removed tray cannot clear a new party's table. Independent incidents create local floor spills. Cleaning does not reset the whole lobby.
- Customers, human players and staff create footprint-shaped floor marks along actual movement. Sampling is every 0.1 service seconds, with a 0.75 metre stride and alternating feet. Idle actors, seated customers, teleports and actors outside the restaurant floor do not produce trails. Repeated traffic darkens nearby marks. Up to 512 marks are retained; new paths replace the faintest marks when full. Eating adds local spills near the table approach.
- Clean now: 5 seconds with kitchen timers paused. Clean while cooking: 20 seconds, new cooking starts take 1.10 times their normal cook duration. Continue: drain already cooking orders, then force a 30-second kitchen clean.
- Reviewed orders accepted during a closure stay in the kitchen queue, using the same order identity and already-committed ingredients. They start after reopening without another submission.
- Staff choices use the authored complaint dialogue presentation. They do not create customer complaints or financial outcomes. Multiplayer host chooses; all peers pause while choosing. Service-action deadlines exclude the pause; network connection/rejoin deadlines continue.
- Purple Kitchen Cleaning progress copies the existing approval row and appears underneath it. The guest projects the host's small hygiene checkpoint between updates.
- A new shift resets dirt. No campaign-save schema or result receipt is changed. Room protocol is casual-session-14; results rules remain casual-session-2. All players must use the updated build.

## Booth and lobby cleaning controls

- Select an empty dirty booth to expose **Clean it yourself** and **Ask staff to clean** using the existing Green/Double rectangle depth-flat sprite. The first action reveals the existing hold-to-clean control; the second requests staff and closes the choices. The two buttons live in the small authored bubble layout, with bounded text sizing. Occupied tables and tables with trays retain their normal service flow. Holding approaches the table and performs its normal cleaning action; releasing cancels. Multiplayer cleanup uses the requesting player's claim lease and validates arrival before committing.
- Staff requests queue one table behind the busser's current task. Duplicate requests do not add jobs. Queued or actively cleaned tables cannot be seated. Unreachable queued tables release after three failed approaches, retaining their dirt.
- Successful table cleaning grants animated white sparkle for 90 scaled service seconds. Another use immediately ends the shine. Fresh tables are clean without sparkle. Dirt tiers are clean below 0.25, slightly dirty from 0.25, dirty from 0.55, and super dirty from 0.8. Slight wear below 0.25 is not rendered on dining surfaces.
- Customer walking marks inherit green, pink or blue; players and staff leave neutral marks. Tap the busser to request lobby cleaning through the existing staff dialogue. Player-facing controls and dialogue call this lobby/floor cleaning.
- A cleaning request captures all existing colors and spills in a nearest-first route. The busser keeps one task through the pass and removes captured marks within 0.8 metres of traveled segments, with a navigation obstruction check. New lobby dirt is suppressed during the active pass. Cleaning work totals 10 scaled service seconds across the pass; normal route travel adds time. Failed approaches rotate to the end and skip after three four-second attempts, leaving their dirt intact and showing one warning. Lobby Cleaning uses the purple HUD through travel and cleaning; when kitchen cleaning overlaps, both rows remain visible. Its completion fade also runs after cancellation.
- The busser uses the supplied mop and rolling bucket models from `Resources/Hygiene/CleaningPresentation`. The mop follows the animated hand grip with a floor-level sweep; the bucket follows recent path corners. Imported model orientation and bounds normalize their size to the staff model. Both props are presentation-only, reused for subsequent passes and hidden on completion, cancellation, role removal or disconnect. The active cleaner does not add new floor marks. Existing decorative scene props remain decorative.
- Once per game-clock hour, an eligible eating customer can comment on their table and nearby floor using the existing thought bubble and mood art. Clean surroundings: “This is a very clean restaurant.” Dirty surroundings: “This can be cleaned more.” Intermediate dirt produces no comment. Feedback adjusts approval by one within the existing daily caps; it does not change payment or satisfied/unsatisfied departure counters.
- Guests receive authoritative dirt, shine, cleaning progress and current feedback. Applying this presentation never performs a cleaning action. Day/run fencing prevents old requests from changing the next shift.

## Integration and authoring

Every restaurant using KitchenManager receives HygieneManager at runtime. The separate legacy hands-on Kitchen tutorial uses a different cooking implementation and does not receive these management-service interruptions.

Current KitchenManager has one shared cooking queue, not per-appliance order routing. Its sanitation gate therefore closes that shared kitchen. This does not introduce a second order scheduler or pretend that orders are assigned to individual appliances.

The five existing service scenes explicitly register 23 kitchen surfaces each, including grills, fryers, stoves, prep surfaces and counters. Booth, long-table and stool prefabs have dining metadata. Runtime registration also recognizes functional stations, sinks and storage components, with mesh names as a compatibility fallback. Kitchen staff's existing visual work visits rotate through matching registered cooking or preparation stations, falling back to authored work points. Only successful visits during active cooking create wear; this does not change cooking timing or create another kitchen scheduler.

Opaque submeshes receive shared URP overlays; transparent submeshes, UI, characters, carried food and restock boxes are excluded. Dirt patterns use world units. Dining wear is more prominent than counter wear. Floors receive a separate bounded footprint mesh, never a tint across the entire floor. Marks use validated restaurant NavMesh positions and physical floor heights where colliders are available.

Add HygieneSurface to an authored prop to select area, surface kind, kitchen use, optional stable ID, spot scale or exclusion. Floor metadata excludes a prop from equipment overlays. Authoring components register newly spawned props on enable; destroyed renderers are removed from the registry. Base materials are preserved. Both shaders live in Resources/Hygiene for build inclusion and generate their patterns procedurally.

Lobby help selects one available dirty tray, otherwise an available dirty booth, and uses the normal claim, movement, cleaning and sink flow. It does not change saved roles. Occupied hands or existing tasks prevent takeover; staff retain their unrelated tasks. Accepting help also schedules the captured floor-cleaning pass described above. Declining help leaves regular service running. Existing work animations are reused; no dedicated mop animation or new equipment asset is introduced.

Multiplayer sends at most 256 surface records and 512 compact floor records at most twice per second for routine activity; decisions and route start/end publish immediately. Snapshot version 5 uses 21 binary bytes per floor record before Base64 encoding, including color and stable mark identity. It also preserves route order, active state, elapsed cleaning work and skipped targets. Invalid or oversized checkpoints are rejected. Guests do not independently generate dirt. Rejoin restores positions, orientation, color, intensity and remaining shine. Surface rendering uses shared material/property blocks; floor rendering uses one dynamic mesh, rebuilt only when its state revision changes. Captured targets cannot be evicted by new traffic at the visual budget limit.

## Validation status

Source and asset-reference checks only on the user's computer. No Unity launch, compiler, build, Play Mode, GUI testing or performance capture was run. The following external gates remain required before declaring gameplay verified.

Run Unity 6000.0.40f1 in a separate test workspace with `-executeMethod HygieneRegressionCases.Run` for model timing, thresholds, pause behavior, serialized rejoin state, shader inclusion and the complaint presentation bindings. This is an opt-in editor entry point; it never runs on asset import.

## Human-control acceptance matrix

1. Start a fresh shift: clean surfaces, no sanitation popup, no cleaning HUD. Repeat on the next day and in a fresh run.
2. Submit orders until the first kitchen warning. Confirm all clients stop clocks, movement and customer patience. Keep the popup open for more than 40 real seconds with in-flight service requests; no service task should time out.
3. Choose clean now during cooking: existing remaining work freezes for 5 service seconds, new orders queue, then each produces one result. Check stock usage, full spawn slots and cancellation by customer departure.
4. Choose clean while cooking: existing forecasts retain their original duration; newly starting work takes 10% longer. After 20 service seconds the kitchen is clean and new work returns to normal timing.
5. Continue until unsanitary: new starts wait; active cooking drains; mandatory cleaning lasts 30 service seconds and the queue resumes. Repeat to ensure the warning rearms.
6. Host and guest complete their own food, bill, cash/change and card tasks before, during and after each cleaning choice. Popup clicks must not issue a world interaction.
7. Request lobby help with empty hands. Verify tray pickup automatically continues to the sink; booth help reaches the booth and completes once. Cancel movement, take another action, destroy the target and disconnect; no stale claim or permanent role change may remain.
8. Test both popup types while another complaint/computer/restock screen is open. They must not stack or expose restaurant UI in the restock view.
9. Test duplicate decisions, guest rejoin during the popup/cleaning, host loss, and day transition. A guest cannot choose the host decision; old snapshots cannot revive an earlier day.
10. Verify mobile portrait/landscape and safe areas: purple progress stays below approval, without overlapping other HUD rows, and fades after completion.
11. Inspect opaque and transparent props, differently sized meshes, disabled objects and authored exclusions in an Android build. Walk customers through the lobby, seat/eat repeatedly, and check that actual floor/table surfaces darken without ceiling/wall stains. Compare host and guest and rejoin. Ask for help: verify staff reach the dirty area and remove only nearby marks. Check LOD/static batching, render order and dirt placement visually.
12. Profile a busy four-player service period on the Android target: sustained 30 FPS, no growing network queue, no per-frame material allocations, and no excessive overlay draw calls. Tune visual eligibility and incident rates only from those measurements.
13. Use one table repeatedly while leaving another unused: only the used table should darken. Exercise grills, fryers, stoves, preparation, pickup and sink interactions. Verify staff can reach registered stations and unsuccessful approaches add no dirt.
14. Walk the same route repeatedly, stand still, teleport, visit restock and rejoin. Only actual restaurant foot traffic leaves tracks; busy routes darken without staining the entire floor. Local floor cleaning preserves distant marks. Verify all initially captured marks are handled and new traffic cannot extend the pass indefinitely.
15. On host and guest, hold/release booth cleaning during approach and work. Race two players, queue staff, remove the role, disconnect and change days. Verify one completion, no stale claim, correct seating exclusion and usable controls afterward.
16. Check white sparkle on booth/long-table/stool dining surfaces, pause its 90-second expiry, reuse the table and rejoin. Confirm fresh tables never sparkle and kitchen equipment never receives this effect.
17. Observe green/pink/blue walking marks and neutral staff/player marks. Request floor cleaning manually; block a route and verify bounded recovery. Inspect simultaneous Kitchen Cleaning and Lobby Cleaning HUD rows on mobile. Verify one mop and bucket, proper hand/floor alignment, corner following and no tool colliders. Confirm all initially reachable marks clear only when the busser passes, no new lobby marks appear during the pass, active work totals 10 service seconds plus travel, and all props/HUD disappear after completion or cancellation.
18. Observe clean and dirty dining feedback across several game hours on all peers. Verify one comment per hour, existing mood/bubble style, daily approval caps, and unchanged departure counters. Delay customer replication; current feedback may appear once when its customer is ready, never replay after expiry.

Future restaurant implementations with a separate cooking pipeline must call the same sanitation hooks before claiming coverage. Kitchen station-specific scheduling and new spill-service interactions are not supplied by the current restaurant code.

## Slower lobby dirt and clean grace

Before Play Mode, use Dine In > Hygiene Settings or select Assets/_Project/Resources/Hygiene/HygieneSettings.asset. This shared Resources asset controls all currently integrated restaurants. Dirt Speed multiplies all sources (0 disables new dirt, 0.5 halves it, 1 preserves the current balance). Separate kitchen/lobby multipliers, walking amount/spacing, dining-stage amounts, incident amounts, floor visibility, kitchen/lobby cleaning times, cooking slowdown, mop radius, booth shine and lobby grace can also be edited there. The runtime manager reads this asset automatically; scene component setup is unnecessary. Change tuning before starting a run and distribute the same asset in all multiplayer builds. Existing dirt is not removed by lowering dirt speed. Safety limits for snapshot sizes and network validation remain code-defined.

Staff hygiene choices use the existing Green/Double asset in all three slots, with green/neutral interaction states. The source customer complaint prefab retains its original response styling.

Walking dirt is 0.0125 per 0.75 metre sample; booth/table wear and lobby incidents use a 0.35 multiplier. Floor marks at or below 0.10 are invisible. Above that threshold opacity grows linearly, avoiding amplification of faint marks. These are tuning defaults, not runtime performance measurements.

All lobby additions are rejected by the state while a floor route is active, including eating spills, human/customer/staff walking and dining wear. Kitchen use remains independent. Walking samples reset during suppression so traffic is not replayed afterward; customer phases continue to be observed so suppressed transitions are not replayed either.

A completed route with no skipped targets grants two in-game hours without new lobby dirt. The authoritative game clock advances this timer; paused clocks do not consume it. Cancellation and partial routes grant no new grace. Existing dirt is not erased by grace. Booth-only cleaning does not grant lobby-wide protection. A new shift resets protection. Snapshot v5 preserves the remaining hours and clock anchor; protocol 14 prevents older clients from joining.

External acceptance additions: compare all hygiene button states; observe one group versus sustained traffic; verify zero new lobby dirt during cleaning and the following two game hours while kitchen use still accumulates; test paused clocks, cancelled/blocked routes, booth-only cleaning, guest rejoin during grace and the next shift. HygieneRegressionCases.Run includes model and asset checks for these cases; Unity execution remains external.
